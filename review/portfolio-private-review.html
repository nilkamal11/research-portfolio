District Learning Context Explorer

Explore district achievement and public finance in context. Companion views cover North Palos-area district spending and Illinois school-level Grade 4 results.

Published explorer · extension in progress

Useful for District analysts, education researchers, and families

The question

How do a district’s estimates compare with districts serving similar communities, and how has the pattern changed over time?

My contribution

Built a Python, SQL, and DuckDB pipeline to reshape source releases, audit identifiers, and prepare browser-ready comparisons. The finance extension reconciles year-specific district identities and source dollar units. Views retain missing years and uncertainty instead of implying a continuous trend.

Data & approach

SEDA 2025.2 administrative-district estimates are shown within a selected grade and subject. Peer selection uses public community context rather than test scores. The Illinois companion uses public Report Card workbooks for 2022–2025 and separates suppression from unavailable measures. The Census F-33 finance view covers FY2022–2024 district-wide spending, keeping current spending separate from capital outlay and documenting source reconciliation issues.

What it delivers

A published SEDA district explorer, a North Palos-area finance view, and a companion Grade 4 school dashboard for North Palos 117, Indian Springs 109, Palos 118, and Worth 127. The broader Illinois rebuild remains in progress.

Interpretation & boundaries

These are descriptive comparisons, not individual-student growth or causal effects. District-wide spending is not fourth-grade spending or an estimate of the effect of spending on achievement. Alternative peer weights have not yet been sensitivity-tested. Illinois changed proficiency thresholds in 2025, so those proficiency rates should not be directly compared with earlier years. Grade 4-only SGP is not available at the same grain in the 2022–2024 files.

Doctoral borrowing

Compare doctoral funding by field and graduate debt across racial and ethnic groups, using checked NCSES tables and interactive D3 views.

Published study

Useful for Education finance researchers and graduate education leaders

The question

Who borrows to complete a research doctorate, and how do funding and debt patterns differ across fields and groups?

My contribution

Wrote Python extraction and source checks across three NCSES workbooks, reconciling debt bands, response denominators, and suppressed cells before building the D3 views.

Data & approach

Uses published tables from the 2024 Survey of Earned Doctorates, an annual census of research doctorate recipients. Each finding traces to workbook cells and its relevant response denominator. Funding and debt remain separate measures.

Selected findings

46.7% of education doctorate recipients named their own resources as their primary support. Graduate debt above $70,000 was reported by 24.5% of psychology recipients and 2.2% of engineering recipients; those debt denominators include recipients with no graduate debt.

Interpretation & boundaries

Descriptive aggregate tables cannot connect an individual’s funding to debt or establish causes. Group comparisons are unadjusted. Missing and suppressed cells remain unavailable; professional practice doctorates are outside the study population.

Doctorate careers

Explore where doctorate holders work and what they do, preserving the relationship between field, employer sector, and primary activity.

Published study

Useful for Graduate education leaders, career researchers, and prospective students

The question

How does employment differ across doctoral fields, employer sectors, and the activities people perform?

My contribution

Built the weighted field-by-sector-by-activity tables and D3 views. Combined small activity groups while retaining respondents in denominators, and kept full-time salary estimates separate from the wider employment population.

Data & approach

Uses the 2023 Survey of Doctorate Recipients public-use file and final survey weights for employed U.S.-residing doctorate holders. The three-way tables describe the same respondents; a separate published salary table supplies medians and standard errors.

Selected findings

An estimated 61.1% of employed engineering doctorate holders worked for private for-profit employers in 2023. Educational institutions employed an estimated 63.3% of employed social science doctorate holders.

Interpretation & boundaries

A cross-sectional employment snapshot does not measure career transitions. Flow estimates have no design-based standard errors or significance tests. Salary estimates cover a different, full-time population; the differences do not estimate causal returns.

NWEA Research Works

Search a saved research-library snapshot by topic, publication type, and contributor, then follow each record back to its source.

Published snapshot explorer

Useful for K–12 researchers and evidence reviewers

The question

How can a research library become easier to search and inspect without losing its source context?

My contribution

Prepared reproducible research metadata and built linked record, category, and contributor views. A companion Python + D3 version separates Python-generated data preparation from the browser interface.

Data & approach

Uses 317 saved NWEA library entries from September 4, 2026. Filters expose record memberships; contributor links count shared credits. GitHub Pages serves a static Python-generated export for the companion, while the Flask API runs locally.

What it delivers

A searchable library explorer with source links, record exports, topic/type comparisons, and a contributor network.

Interpretation & boundaries

An independent portfolio project, not an official NWEA product or live feed. Entries can be versions or summaries of the same study. Shared credits do not prove collaboration, and posted dates are not verified original publication dates.

Institution Atlas

A University of Delaware pilot that brings institutional and research sources together while keeping identifiers, reporting boundaries, and release vintages visible.

Published prototype

Useful for Institutional research and research administration teams

The question

What can public sources tell us about one institution when their reporting periods and organizational boundaries differ?

My contribution

Built structured source contracts, an identifier crosswalk, reproducible static pages, reconciliation exports, and comparisons across retained vintages.

Data & approach

Combines public institutional, research, award, and publication sources through reviewed identifiers. Source grain, period, release, suppression, and boundary decisions remain explicit rather than treating every source as the same unit.

What it delivers

A public single-institution prototype with source-aware profiles, peer views, downloadable structured records, and an evidence trail.

Interpretation & boundaries

This is a pilot, with no claim of institutional adoption. Related-organization boundary decisions remain pending. Four broader or unresolved HERD peer records are excluded from the median. Source coverage and periods differ across pages.

K–12 Data Library

A searchable guide to 66 public K–12 sources across 20 families, covering access, identifiers, population, missing data, and comparison notes.

Published source guide

Useful for K–12 analysts and researchers choosing public data

The question

Which public source fits an education question, and what should an analyst check before using it?

My contribution

Organized public documentation into a consistent catalog so access rules, geography, record level, releases, and reuse terms can be compared before analysis begins.

Data & approach

All 66 source descriptions were checked against official documentation on September 13, 2026; small samples were checked for 12 entries. Structured catalog records drive the searchable interface.

What it delivers

A reusable source-selection reference with official links and notes on identifiers, changes over time, and missingness.

Interpretation & boundaries

This directory describes sources; it does not contain the underlying datasets or deliver live data. Documentation review is broader than sample-data inspection. Analysts should verify the release and terms needed for their own use.

Higher Ed Data Library

A reference to 90 higher education datasets in 20 source groups, with permanent records, catalog exports, and release comparison checks.

Published source guide

Useful for Higher education analysts and institutional researchers

The question

How can a dataset directory be useful to people and reusable by analytical workflows?

My contribution

Built a catalog-driven publication with stable dataset IDs, complete HTML records, JSON and CSV exports, a schema, retained snapshots, and change reports.

Data & approach

All 90 records were checked against official documentation on September 13, 2026; 22 also had a bounded sample inspected. A build-time renderer keeps the full directory readable without JavaScript.

What it delivers

A searchable source guide with stable dataset pages and reusable catalog exports, supported by automated build and link checks.

Interpretation & boundaries

A directory of datasets, not a live data feed or underlying data warehouse. Automated availability checks do not mean every variable, year, or geography has been manually reviewed. State and system coverage is illustrative.

MyTradePathway

A browser-only career-pathway demo with student, counselor, and administrator journeys, practice activities, and local progress.

Functional synthetic demo

Useful for Career education teams and product reviewers

The question

How could a student move from exploring trades to choosing a pathway and preparing for next steps?

My contribution

Built representative journeys for trade exploration, preparation, practice, counselor reporting, and sample content review, using synthetic accounts and browser-local state.

Data & approach

The public demo includes ten trades, twelve supplied local program records, and 135 original practice questions. Synthetic student and activity fixtures support role-based demonstrations; progress stays in the current browser.

What it delivers

A functional product demonstration with locally generated reports and an offline public demo shell.

Interpretation & boundaries

Roles simulate access; there is no live authentication, payment, shared state, or server API. People and activity are synthetic. Supplied wages, fees, eligibility, and deadlines are unverified, and some windows have closed. It demonstrates a product concept rather than measured student outcomes.
