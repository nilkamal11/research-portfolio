How I work

Make the data
make sense.

My work connects research, data preparation, and interactive reporting across K–12 and higher education. I use Python, SQL, and visual interfaces to make the source, population, and limits of an analysis easier to understand.

I’m interested in the work between a promising question and a defensible answer: aligning definitions, checking denominators, handling missingness, and building something another person can use.

01

Start with the source

Keep reporting periods, identifiers, populations, and release vintages explicit.

02

Build for exploration

Connect the research question to clear views, useful filters, and downloadable records.

03

Leave a path to reuse

Document preparation, methods, and boundaries so the next analyst can follow the work.

Research on
student borrowing

Indebted Over Time: Racial Differences in Student Borrowing ↗

Educational Researcher, 2019, 48(8), 558–563.

Examines changes in federal student borrowing across racial and ethnic groups using five NPSAS administrations from 2000 through 2016.

I was the data person on this research team and a coauthor of the paper. I built analysis datasets from five waves of the National Postsecondary Student Aid Study, reconciling changing race categories and other definitions before discussing results with the team.

National Trends in Federal Student Loan Borrowing by Income Group and First-Generation Status ↗

AIR Professional File, Spring 2020, article 148. DOI: 10.34315/apf1482020.

Examines federal borrowing patterns by family income and parental education using nationally representative NPSAS data.

I am a coauthor. My contribution included preparing analysis datasets, harmonizing changing survey variables, and discussing results with the research team.
