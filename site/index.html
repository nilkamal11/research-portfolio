Doctoral borrowing

In 2024, 24.5% of psychology doctorate recipients who answered the graduate-debt question owed more than $70,000. In engineering, it was 2.2%. I also compare funding by field and graduate debt across racial and ethnic groups.

I reconciled three NCSES tables in Python and built the D3 views; conflicting definitions meant leaving median debt out. With approved restricted-use SED access, I’d examine adjusted differences in debt within fields.

Doctorate careers

Private for-profit employers accounted for an estimated 61.1% of employed engineering doctorate holders living in the U.S. in 2023.

My work here was the weighted tables and D3 views, including keeping salaries separate when their full-time population could not be reproduced from the public file. A next project would use approved longitudinal SDR access to track sector changes for the same people across survey waves.

Related research

Student borrowing over time

Indebted Over Time: Racial Differences in Student Borrowing

Educational Researcher, 2019, 48(8), 558–563.

I was the data person on this research team and a coauthor of the paper. I built the analysis datasets from five waves of the National Postsecondary Student Aid Study (NPSAS), then brought the results back to the team for discussion.

Race categories and other variables changed between rounds, so comparing borrowing over time meant working out which definitions lined up and recoding the data consistently. The doctoral borrowing project brought me back to that kind of work: checking who a table actually counts before comparing the numbers.
